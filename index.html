How to Spot Crypto Giveaway Scams

Crypto giveaway scams are one of the most common ways people lose funds. The following are the warning signs the Vantom Labs community recommends you verify before connecting any wallet to any site.

Offers that feel too generous

Legitimate programs cap rewards at realistic amounts. If a site offers tens or hundreds of SOL for a single click, the numbers themselves are the warning sign — real distributions are sized to a budget and a per-wallet cap.

Requests for seed phrases or private keys

No legitimate site, wallet, exchange, or giveaway will ever ask for your seed phrase, private key, or recovery phrase. Anyone who does is attempting to steal your entire wallet. Close the page immediately.

Pressure and countdown urgency

Scams create artificial scarcity — a countdown that never runs out, a "limited allocation" with no total, an offer that "expires in minutes." Real programs show a budget, a cycle end, and a way to verify both.

Unknown token approvals

The most dangerous request is an approval, not a transfer. When you approve a token to an unknown spender, that spender can move the token at any time in the future. Read every approval carefully. Revoke approvals you do not recognize using a trusted revoke tool.

Look-alike domains

Clone sites often use domains that resemble the real one by a character or two. Bookmark the official domain once and always return through the bookmark — never through a link from a DM, ad, or search result.

Wallet connection is not always harmless

Connecting a wallet is safe. Signing a transaction is not. Read every transaction prompt carefully. If you do not recognize the destination address or the intent, do not sign.

If you believe you have interacted with a fraudulent site, revoke any approvals, move remaining assets to a fresh wallet, and report the domain to your wallet provider.
